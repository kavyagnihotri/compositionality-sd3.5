\begin{abstract}
Modern text-to-image diffusion models struggle with compositional reasoning because standard text encoders lack strict spatial grounding. In this work, we investigate whether targeted text-encoder interventions can resolve these spatial deficits without the prohibitive computational cost of full-model retraining. We perform a modular ``weight surgery'' on Stable Diffusion 3.5 Large Turbo (SD3.5), replacing its default CLIP-L encoder with two compositionally-aware variants: CLIC RedCaps and CLIC CogVLM. Evaluating across a standardized benchmark of over 1,300 relational prompts using Qwen3-VL-30B and manual annotation, we demonstrate some improvements in spatial accuracy; particularly in complex directional and containment tasks. On the GenEval2 Soft-TIFA benchmark (800 prompts), both CLIC variants improve over the Turbo baseline, with CogVLM reaching the highest atom-level score ($56.52\%$ AM vs.\ $55.48\%$ baseline) and RedCaps the highest prompt-level consistency ($10.68\%$ GM vs.\ $8.91\%$ baseline).  Furthermore, our error analysis uncovers a ``Reasoning-Output Paradox'' within automated VLM evaluators and highlights the persistent dominance of semantic priors over structural nouns. Ultimately, our findings establish that visual compositionality relies heavily on text-encoder grounding rather than mere model scale, providing a highly efficient framework to enhance spatial reliability in production-ready diffusion pipelines.
\end{abstract}
